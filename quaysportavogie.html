The Ards Peninsula

Portavogie

A working fishing port on the easternmost edge of Ireland — and the reason our menu looks the way it does.

Portavogie is a village and fishing port in County Down, on the east coast of the Ards Peninsula. It is the easternmost settlement in Ireland, and it faces the Irish Sea.

The name comes from the Irish Port a’ Bhogaigh, meaning “harbour of the bog”. A settlement was established nearby at Stable Hole in the mid-sixteenth century, sheltered by rocks and a sandy shore where boats could be beached.

The modern harbour was developed in the 1980s and opened in 1985. It houses a large fishing fleet catching mainly prawns and herring, and most evenings there are fish auctions on the quays.

The prawn

Why Portavogie is on menus worldwide

The Portavogie prawn — the langoustine, also known as nephrops or the Dublin Bay prawn — is the village’s best-known export. Whole, or as scampi made from the tails, it appears on restaurant menus far beyond County Down.

They are caught by trawlers and potting boats around the County Down coast, and look like a small, pale-pink lobster with long thin claws.

Each August the village holds a Seafood Festival on the harbour to mark it.

Nearby

Things to do around Portavogie

The harbour

The working harbour sits at the heart of the village and can be seen closely from the harbour car park. Weekends are the best time to find it full of boats. Seals swim into the harbour regularly.

The beaches

East Shore is a good walking beach with a promenade and a children’s playground beside the car park. South Shore, on the other side of the harbour, looks out to Kilwarlin Lighthouse on South Rock, Green Isle, and the tern colony on Bird Isle.

McCammon Rocks

A reef just off the shore. At low tide you can walk out to it. Fishermen once anchored there; today it is a reliable place to see seals.

The George Best mural

There is a mural of George Best just along from the harbour — and the café in our own building, Besties, takes its name from him.

Kirkistown Race Track

A little inland, on a former RAF airfield, hosting car and motorcycle racing through the summer.

Further along the peninsula

Portaferry and the entrance to Strangford Lough lie to the south; Ballywalter and Donaghadee to the north. The Ards Peninsula coast road links them all.

Please check opening times and access with each attraction before travelling.

Questions

About Portavogie

What is Portavogie known for?

Portavogie is a fishing village and working harbour in County Down. It is the easternmost settlement in Ireland and is best known for the Portavogie prawn — the langoustine, also called nephrops or the Dublin Bay prawn — as well as herring. Fish auctions are held on the quays most evenings.

Where is Portavogie?

On the east coast of the Ards Peninsula in County Down, Northern Ireland, facing the Irish Sea. It sits within the Ards and North Down Borough, around seven miles from Portaferry and close to Strangford Lough.

What is there to do near Portavogie?

Two beaches — East Shore with its promenade and playground, and South Shore looking out to Green Isle and Bird Isle. McCammon Rocks can be walked to at low tide and seals gather there. Kirkistown Race Track is a short drive inland, and there is a George Best mural near the harbour.

Where can I eat in Portavogie?

The New Quays is a seafood restaurant and bar at 81 New Harbour Road, about a hundred yards from the harbour. Besties Café, in the same building, serves breakfast and brunch. Tables are booked by phone on 028 4277 2225.
